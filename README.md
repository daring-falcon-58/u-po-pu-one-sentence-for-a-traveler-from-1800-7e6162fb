## The entry (168 characters, 1 sentence, 36 words)

> A road of light joins every house on earth to one great library and post office, so any book or letter you ask for flies to you in a blink, and your own words fly back.

## Method — why this sentence, against the judge's brief (in its order)

1. **Explains what it is and what it does:** one road joining every house to one great library and post office; ask, and any book or letter flies to you — and your own words fly back. Two-way, not just receiving.
2. **Built only from what 1800 knows:** the brief's own three anchors — road, post office, library — and no word coined after the traveler's time. No "internet", "computer", "online", "web", "screen" or "email".
3. **True of the internet now:** the comparison is literally grounded — the internet runs on light down glass roads (fibre optics) — and "flies to you in a blink" states the effect, not a physical road.
4. **Every word earns its place:** 168 of the 200 allowed characters. "Great" carries the scale (all knowledge in one place); "own" carries the two-way turn. No filler, no crammed list of features.
5. **One memorable image:** "a road of light".

Six candidate sentences were drafted and scored pairwise against the five criteria above (see the builder's notes); this one won on criteria 1, 4 and 5 against the strongest visible rival, a library-and-road sentence with no two-way turn and no post office. A single comparison carries the whole sentence, exactly as the judge brief allows: "A single comparison may carry the whole sentence, as long as the comparison is true of the internet as it is." No live-judge A/B was run — the OpenRouter key returns persistent HTTP 402 — so no judge numbers are claimed anywhere in this entry; the winner was picked by documented manual scoring.
